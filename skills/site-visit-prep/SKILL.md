# Site Visit Prep

## Workflow

### Step 1: Load Standards and Set the Meeting

Load the `repe-core` skill and follow `references/standards.md`.
Identify the property type and read its playbook in `references/playbooks/`.

Then confirm:

| Item | Content |
|------|---------|
| Meeting type | Property tour, PM interview, broker call, lender call or seller meeting |
| Date, time and attendees | |
| Deal stage | Screening, bidding, under contract or post-closing |
| Prior outputs available | Screening memo, rent roll analysis, market brief, diligence tracker |

### Step 2: Gather Open Items

Pull every unresolved point from the prior outputs into one list.

| Open item | Source | Why it matters | Who can answer |
|-----------|--------|----------------|----------------|

- From `deal-screening`: first-call questions and data gaps
- From `rent-roll-t12-analysis`: unexplained variances and normalization questions
- From `market-intelligence`: red flags and site visit questions
- From `dd-checklist`: items with status Issue or overdue

### Step 3: Write Questions by Audience

Draw on the playbook's site visit and management questions, then add the open items routed to the person who can answer them.

| Audience | Focus |
|----------|-------|
| Property manager | Leasing traffic, renewals, delinquency, turn times, staffing, vendor issues, resident or tenant complaints |
| Broker | Seller motive, other bidders, pricing guidance, process and timing, what has been disclosed elsewhere |
| Lender | Sizing test that binds, rate and spread, reserves, conditions, timing to commitment |
| Seller | Reasons for selling, capital spent and deferred, known issues, flexibility on terms |

Keep each list to the eight to ten questions that move value or risk. Put the most important first.

### Step 4: Plan What to Observe

| Area | What to look for |
|------|------------------|
| Exterior and site | Roofs, paving, drainage, signage, lighting, parking counts |
| Building systems | Age and condition of HVAC, boilers, elevators, electrical rooms |
| Units or suites | Condition of vacant and occupied space, renovated vs classic, down units |
| Common areas and amenities | Upkeep, use, what competitors offer |
| Neighborhood | Traffic and access, visibility, nearby uses, crime signals |
| Competing properties | Drive by the top rent comps and note condition and pricing |

Red flags to probe:

| Red flag | What to look for or ask | Evidence that would close it |
|----------|------------------------|------------------------------|

### Step 5: Output

- Two-page prep sheet: meeting purpose, top three things to learn, questions by audience, observation checklist, red flags with closing evidence.
- Blank notes template: one section per question and area, space for answers, photos to take, follow-up owner and date.
- After the visit, feed answers back into `dd-checklist` and the model assumptions, and add a decision record per the standards.

## Important Notes

- Walk the units the seller did not pick. Ask for the vacant and down unit list in advance and choose a sample yourself.
- Property managers who are leaving with the sale often speak most plainly. Who stays and who goes shapes what they tell you.
- Ask open questions first and confirm numbers second. Leading with the rent roll lets the answer fit the document.
- Visit the competition the same day. Condition and pricing look different side by side.
- Note smells, water stains and recent patch paint. They point to the problems the condition report must explain.
- Write notes within the hour. Details fade fast, and the team acts on what is written.
