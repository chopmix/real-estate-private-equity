# Land and development playbook

Engine: development layer. Applies to ground-up and heavy redevelopment of any income type. Read this file together with the playbook for the income type being built, which supplies stabilized rent, expenses and exit cap.

## 1. What it is

| Subtype | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Entitled land | Zoning and approvals in hand. Pay for certainty |
| Unentitled land | Approval risk and time. Option or contingent contract is common |
| Ground-up build | Land, design, permits, construction, lease-up, stabilization |
| Heavy redevelopment | Gut renovation, change of use or major addition. Treat like new construction |
| Build to suit | Tenant signed before start. Lower lease-up risk, tenant credit risk |

## 2. How income is produced

- No income during construction. Money goes out against a budget, in draws.
- After completion, the project leases up and reaches stabilized NOI. The profit is the spread between value at stabilization and total cost.
- The test is yield on cost against the exit cap rate. Spread is the margin for risk.
- Interest and operating deficits during lease-up are real costs and belong in the budget.

## 3. Key metrics

| Metric | How to compute | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| Total project cost | Land plus hard, soft, financing, contingency and lease-up costs | Basis |
| Yield on cost | Stabilized NOI / total project cost | Core return before exit |
| Development spread | Yield on cost minus exit cap rate | Margin for risk. Rule of thumb, verify locally: often 100 to 200 basis points or more |
| Profit on cost | (Stabilized value minus total cost) / total cost | Return on the build |
| Cost per unit or SF | Total cost / units or SF, compared with the market price | Check against existing buildings |
| Contingency remaining | Contingency budget minus used, compared with percent complete | Early warning of overruns |
| Cost to complete | Remaining budget plus known overruns minus committed funding | Funding gap |
| Percent complete, billed vs physical | Payments / budget, against inspector's progress | Checks draws are earned |
| Schedule variance | Planned vs actual completion by milestone | Interest and rent loss |
| Peak equity | Maximum cumulative equity outstanding | True equity need |

## 4. Revenue drivers

- Stabilized rent, from the income type's playbook. Test against competing new buildings, not old stock.
- Lease-up pace: units or SF absorbed per month. Slower pace costs interest and concessions.
- Concessions during lease-up. Show effective rent, not just face rent.
- Pre-leasing or tenant commitments before start.
- Product fit: unit mix, amenities, parking, and floor plate for the target tenant.

## 5. Expense drivers

- Construction cost is the main driver. See capital needs.
- Property tax during construction and after completion. Model on stabilized value where reassessed.
- Operating expenses during lease-up, since income is partial.
- Insurance during construction: builders risk and liability. Check coverage and deductibles.
- Operating costs at stabilization, from the income type's playbook.

## 6. Capital needs

- Budget by category: land, site work, hard costs, soft costs (design, permits, legal, fees), financing costs, developer fee, contingency, lease-up reserve, tenant improvements and leasing commissions if commercial.
- Contingency: separate hard cost and soft cost contingency. Rule of thumb, verify locally. Contingency usually rises with complexity and early design stage.
- Impact fees, utility connections, off-site improvements. Check who pays.
- Interest reserve and operating deficit reserve through stabilization.
- Draw schedule: monthly draws against progress, with retainage held by the lender and owner.
- Equity goes in first, then debt. State the order and the amount.

## 7. Debt and lender view

- Lenders: banks (mostly), debt funds, life companies for take-out. Construction lending is the most tightly underwritten.
- Sizing: loan to cost, loan to stabilized value, and debt yield on stabilized NOI. The lowest binds.
- Typical terms: equity first, interest reserve, completion test, cost-to-complete test, retainage, inspector.
- Guarantees: completion guarantee, cost overrun guarantee, interest and carry guarantee, repayment guarantee. Identify who gives them, and for how long. Flag for counsel.
- Modifications: extension tests (occupancy, DSCR, debt yield), minimum liquidity, net worth.
- Permanent take-out: confirm the stabilized loan sizing before the construction loan closes.

## 8. Screening quick tests

| Test | Pass looks like |
|------|-----------------|
| Yield on cost vs exit cap | Spread meets the firm's minimum, with a margin after downside cases |
| Cost per unit or SF vs market | At or below price of existing comparable buildings |
| Entitlements | Complete, final and not appealable, or priced as an option |
| Contingency | Right size for design stage, with a clear owner of savings and overruns |
| Land basis | Supported by residual land value, not by seller ask |
| Lease-up time | Based on competing deliveries, not the sponsor's best case |
| Peak equity | Within the fund's capacity, with a reserve for overrun |

## 9. Diligence additions

- Entitlements: zoning, site plan approval, variances, development agreements, vested rights, conditions of approval, expiry dates, appeal periods. Flag for counsel.
- Title and survey, easements, access, encroachments, utilities, and flood zone.
- Environmental reports and geotechnical report. Soil, groundwater, wetlands, endangered species, contamination.
- Utilities capacity letters and fees. Who pays for off-site upgrades.
- Plans and specifications completeness: percent complete, owner review, value engineering.
- Construction contract: guaranteed maximum price or fixed price, fee, general conditions, allowances, bonding, retainage, liquidated damages, change order process.
- Contractor: financials, backlog, similar projects, references, key people, subcontractor default insurance.
- Budget review by a third party. Line by line.
- Schedule review: critical path, long-lead items, permits, weather risk.
- Developer: track record, completed deals, litigation, financial capacity, who stands behind guarantees.
- Pre-leasing, tenant letters of intent, or anchor commitments.

## 10. Red flags

- Entitlements not final, or conditions not yet met.
- Budget built from an early design, with contingency that is thin or already spent.
- Land bought at a price that leaves no margin.
- Contractor under financial strain, or a bid with high allowances.
- Draw requests ahead of physical progress, or heavy front-loaded general conditions.
- Rising contingency use while percent complete is low.
- Yield on cost spread under the firm's minimum, or driven by aggressive rent.
- Lease-up plan that ignores competing deliveries.
- Guarantees given by a developer without the net worth to stand behind them.
- No take-out loan sizing test or a loan that matures before stabilization.

## 11. Value levers

- Buy land with entitlements in hand, or take approvals risk through an option.
- Improve the design: efficiency, parking, unit mix.
- Value engineering before the contract is signed.
- Buy out trades early and lock prices.
- Phase the project to reduce peak equity and carry.
- Pre-lease or pre-sell to cut risk.
- Hold or sell depending on the spread to exit cap rate.

## 12. Local intelligence

- Building permits and planning agendas: entitlements news, public hearings, neighbor opposition, council votes, moratoria and rule changes.
- Competing pipeline: projects under construction and planned, by unit or SF, delivery date, and pace of lease-up of the ones already open. Absorption of comparable new buildings is the best test of the lease-up plan.
- Land sales and construction cost trends from contractors and local brokers.

## 13. Model inputs to capture

- Budget by category: land, site, hard, soft, financing, developer fee, lease-up reserve, tenant costs.
- Hard and soft cost contingency percent. Escalation percent.
- Schedule: closing, entitlement, start, completion, stabilization by month.
- Draw schedule by month, retainage percent, equity first or pro rata.
- Lease-up or absorption per month, concessions, pre-leasing.
- Stabilized rent and expense inputs from the income type's playbook.
- Property tax during construction and at stabilization.
- Construction loan: amount, rate, fees, interest reserve, extension terms. Take-out loan: size, rate.
- Exit cap rate, sale cost, hold period, yield on cost, and development spread.
- Guarantee terms and fees.

## 14. Site visit and management questions

- Walk the site: access, utilities, slope, neighbors, drainage, nearby competing buildings.
- If under construction: compare physical progress against the draw request.
- Ask the developer which items in the budget are still estimates and which are bought out.
- Ask what is not in the budget: off-site, impact fees, utilities, furniture, pre-opening.
- Ask how the contractor handles change orders and who approves them.
- Ask what would delay entitlement or permits, and who is working on each approval.
- Ask what the lease-up plan assumes and which competing buildings it was tested against.
- Ask who gives the guarantees and what they are worth.
- Ask what happens if cost rises 10 percent or the start slips six months.
