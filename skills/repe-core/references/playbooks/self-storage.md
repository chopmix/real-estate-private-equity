# Self-storage playbook

Engine: operating. Customers rent by the month on short agreements, so income behaves like a daily-priced business with very low expense per dollar of revenue.

## 1. What it is

| Subtype | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Climate-controlled, multistory | Higher rate per SF, higher cost to build, strong in dense markets |
| Drive-up, single story | Lower cost, lower rate, wider trade area |
| Mixed (climate and drive-up) | Most common. Unit mix drives revenue |
| Vehicle, boat and RV | Outdoor parking, very low cost, seasonal |
| Conversion (retail or warehouse to storage) | Lower basis, check the building for fit |

## 2. How income is produced

- Customers sign month-to-month rental agreements, so every unit re-prices continuously.
- Revenue is rent by unit size and type, plus fees, insurance or protection plan income, retail sales (locks, boxes) and ancillary income.
- Management is typically a third party or an owner platform with its own web presence, call center and pricing system.
- Existing customers are charged increases over time. New customers pay current street rates, often discounted.

## 3. Key metrics

| Metric | How to compute | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| Physical occupancy | Occupied SF / net rentable SF (also by unit count) | Space used |
| Economic occupancy | Collected rent / rent at street rates on all units | Revenue earned against potential |
| Rent per SF | Annual rent / occupied SF | Pricing level |
| Street rate vs in-place rate | Current advertised rate / rate paid by existing customers | Size of the existing customer discount or premium |
| Net rentable SF per capita | Competing storage SF in trade area / population | Supply saturation |
| Move-ins and move-outs | Units rented and vacated per month | Demand pulse |
| Average length of stay | Months a customer keeps a unit | Churn and cost of acquisition |
| Delinquency and auction rate | Past due rent / billed rent. Units sold at lien sale / units | Credit quality and collection discipline |
| Expense ratio | Operating expense / effective gross income | Efficiency. Low by nature |
| Price per SF | Purchase price / net rentable SF, compared with replacement cost | Basis check |

## 4. Revenue drivers

- Net rentable SF and unit mix: size, climate or not, floor, access.
- Street rate by unit type, and promotions (first month free, discount).
- Annual and periodic increases to existing customers. Test how many customers leave after an increase.
- Occupancy trend by unit type. Small units often fill first and are priced highest per SF.
- Fees: admin fee, late fee, lien fee. Check legality and local limits.
- Tenant protection and insurance income. Confirm how it is shared with any third party.
- Retail sales, truck rental commissions, parking, and cell tower income.
- Marketing: online search position, review volume, and branded website.

## 5. Expense drivers

- Payroll: a small on-site team, or a remote call center. Count real hours.
- Marketing and online advertising, often a large line. Understand cost per rental.
- Management fee percent of revenue, plus call center and platform fees.
- Property tax: reassessment on sale is a common surprise, since storage is valued on income. Model it.
- Insurance, utilities (higher with climate control), repairs, software, credit card fees.
- Seller adjustments: owner-operated facilities often show no payroll or no management fee. Replace with market costs.

## 6. Capital needs

- Replacement reserve per SF, rule of thumb, verify locally.
- Roofs, doors, paving, gates, access control, cameras, lighting.
- Unit door and hallway replacement for older buildings.
- Expansion on excess land, or conversion of unused space. Cost per new net rentable SF.
- Lease-up carrying cost for new or expanded buildings, including marketing and operating deficit.

## 7. Debt and lender view

- Lenders: banks, life companies, debt funds, agency programs, CMBS and specialty lenders.
- Sizing: debt yield and DSCR usually bind. Lenders discount NOI from lease-up and use a market management fee.
- Lenders may require several years of operating history. Newly built facilities need a lease-up reserve or guarantee.
- Quirks: assignment of third-party management agreement, treatment of insurance income, environmental reports, rate cap on floating loans.

## 8. Screening quick tests

| Test | Pass looks like |
|------|-----------------|
| Price per SF against replacement cost | Below new build, with a reason new supply is not cheaper |
| Economic vs physical occupancy | Close. Large gaps point to discounting or delinquency |
| In-place rate vs street rate | Understand the gap and whether it can be closed |
| Competing SF per capita | Not oversupplied. Pipeline checked |
| Expense ratio | In line with similar facilities after market payroll and fee |
| Trailing 12-month revenue trend | Stable or rising. Explain any decline |
| Property tax | Modeled on reassessed value |

## 9. Diligence additions

- Management software export: rent roll by unit, rate history, move-in date, rate of increases, autopay and insurance status.
- Trailing 24 months of occupancy, move-ins, move-outs and street rates.
- Ledger reconciliation: rent roll vs bank deposits vs revenue in the T-12.
- Aging of receivables, lien and auction history, and lien process compliance. Flag for counsel.
- Website analytics: traffic, search ranking, cost per lead, conversion rate.
- Marketing contracts, call center contract, third-party insurance contract.
- Zoning, certificate of occupancy, fire code, sprinkler and access control.
- Environmental review: tenants may store hazardous items. Site history, tanks, runoff.
- Physical condition: roofs, doors, paving, drainage, gates, cameras, elevator.
- Unit audit: count a sample of units, check for non-paying, vacant or employee-used units.

## 10. Red flags

- Physical occupancy high but rent per SF below peers: discounting to fill.
- Large gap between street rate and in-place rate that is not being closed.
- Rising delinquency and growing auction pipeline.
- New competitor under construction within the trade area.
- Occupancy supported by free rent or employee units.
- Seller revenue that does not tie to bank deposits.
- No payroll or fee in seller's expenses.
- Weak online presence: low review scores, poor search placement.
- Flood or drainage problems, roof leaks and door damage.

## 11. Value levers

- Move to professional management and revenue management software.
- Raise rates on existing customers in steps, with a track record of churn tests.
- Reduce discounting and trim unproductive promotions.
- Add climate control, extra units or an expansion on excess land.
- Add tenant protection and insurance income.
- Improve online presence and reviews.
- Cut expense: payroll model, utility use, marketing efficiency.

## 12. Local intelligence

- Online reviews on maps and search sites: score trend, volume, owner responses, patterns on gate problems, billing surprises, rate increases, cleanliness, and staff. Read patterns, never name staff or reviewers.
- Competitor web pages: street rates by size, promotions, and unit availability as a proxy for occupancy. Capture the date.
- Pipeline: building permits, site plans, zoning agendas and land sales for new facilities.
- Population growth, apartment supply, home sales, student and military housing, and small business activity.

## 13. Model inputs to capture

- Net rentable SF, unit count, unit mix by size and type, year built.
- Physical and economic occupancy by year. Street rate by unit type, in-place rate by type.
- Annual rate increase on existing customers. Discounts and concessions.
- Other revenue: fees, insurance and protection, retail, parking, cell tower, as percent of rent.
- Departmental and undistributed expense percent of revenue: payroll, marketing, utilities, repairs, software, credit card fees, insurance.
- Management fee percent and platform fees. Bad debt percent.
- Property tax on reassessed value.
- Reserve per SF. Capital plan: roofs, doors, paving, expansion, with timing.
- Lease-up schedule for new or expanded space.
- Exit cap rate, sale cost, hold period.
- Debt: amount, rate, interest-only period, amortization.

## 14. Site visit and management questions

- Visit at different times, on a weekend and on a weekday. Count cars and customers.
- Walk every building, test doors, lifts, lighting and gates. Look at vacant units.
- Ask the manager how many calls and walk-ins turn into rentals, and what happens when someone asks for a discount.
- Ask how often existing customer rates are raised and what happens after.
- Ask how many units are non-paying, in auction process, or used by staff.
- Ask what new competitors are being built and what competitors charge.
- Ask what the largest customer complaints are.
- Ask how many online reviews come from customers and who responds.
