# Playbook: Program Overlays

Use with a property-type playbook. Each program adds rules on top of the base deal. Every tax or legal point here is a flag for a CPA or counsel, not a conclusion. Do not state eligibility, tax outcomes or legal results as fact.

## Opportunity Zones (OZ)

### What it is
- A federal tax program that gives investors tax treatment on capital gains put into a Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF) that invests in property in designated tracts.
- Flag for CPA: investor eligibility, gain timing, fund and entity structure, and holding period benefits. Program rules and designations change, so confirm the current law.

### What changes in underwriting
- Hold period is tied to the program holding period, so model the hold the investor needs, not the sponsor's preferred hold. Flag for CPA.
- Exit date and sale timing affect investor tax outcomes. Show returns before investor tax and state that tax is not modeled.
- Substantial improvement test applies to acquired property. The improvement budget must meet the test inside the allowed period. Flag for counsel and CPA.
- Land value may not count toward the test. Allocate price between land and building with support.
- Model two cases: investor stays to the program hold, and an early exit without program benefit.
- Fund structure adds fees, reporting and legal cost. Include them.

### Diligence additions
- Confirm the tract is a designated zone from the official list, and the date of designation.
- Fund documents, testing dates, and asset test calculations. Counsel and CPA to review.
- Original use or substantial improvement analysis, with an allocation of land and building.
- Construction schedule against the improvement period.
- Business or property use in the tract, and the share of tangible property that qualifies.
- Investor basis, gain source and timing, from the investor's own advisor.

### Red flags
- Deal only works if the tax benefit holds, with weak real estate returns before tax.
- Improvement budget barely meets the test, with no contingency.
- Sponsor cannot hold to the required period.
- Cash flow forced out in ways that risk fund compliance.
- Tract at the edge of a boundary, or designation uncertainty.
- Marketing built on tax claims with no property case.

### Exit effects
- Buyer pool may narrow if the buyer needs program benefit or a clean fund structure.
- Early sale loses investor treatment. Show the investor the cost of exit before the program hold.
- Disposal timing and distribution mechanics need CPA review.

## HUD/FHA Debt

### What it is
- Federally insured multifamily loans made through approved lenders, including programs for new construction, substantial rehab, acquisition, refinance and affordable housing.
- Flag for counsel: program eligibility, use agreement terms, and the effect of regulatory agreements.

### What changes in underwriting
- Long term, fixed rate and high leverage are common, with slow processing. Model closing delay and carry cost.
- Sizing uses a coverage ratio, loan to value or cost, and program limits. State which binds.
- Mortgage insurance premium and fees add to the cost of debt. Include them in the loan constant.
- Loans are usually assumable only with approval, and prepayment cost can be high. Model lockout and prepayment.
- Use agreements, reserves and replacement escrows reduce distributable cash. Model them below NOI.
- Distributions are limited to surplus cash, tested on a schedule. Model timing.
- Construction and rehab loans carry a draw process, cost certification and contractor requirements.

### Diligence additions
- Lender term sheet, program type and timeline.
- Regulatory agreement and use agreement drafts. Counsel to read.
- Third-party reports: appraisal, environmental, property condition and market study.
- Davis-Bacon or labor wage requirements for rehab and construction, if they apply. Counsel to review.
- Senior lender consent requirements on transfer, refinance and sale.
- Prior regulatory findings on the property or sponsor.

### Red flags
- Underwriting depends on a closing date that the program cannot meet.
- Prepayment lockout beyond the planned hold, with no assumption path.
- Sponsor net worth or liquidity short of program rules.
- Hidden cost of fees, escrows and reserves not in the model.
- Distribution limits that break the investor return profile.

### Exit effects
- Buyer must assume the loan or repay it, and either path has cost and delay.
- Assumption gives a financing advantage if the loan rate is below market, but needs approval.
- Reserves and escrows are returned only when released by the lender.

## Agency Debt (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac)

### What it is
- Multifamily loans delivered through approved lenders under federal housing finance agency programs, including conventional, small balance, affordable, student, single-family rental and manufactured housing variants.
- Flag for counsel: loan documents, recourse carve-outs, and any affordability restrictions.

### What changes in underwriting
- Sizing is the lowest of LTV, DSCR and debt yield. Use lender NOI with a market management fee and reserves.
- Interest-only periods, supplemental loans, and floating rate options differ by program. Model each.
- Prepayment is usually yield maintenance or defeasance. Model the cost against the planned hold.
- Reserves and escrows for taxes, insurance and repairs reduce cash flow.
- Affordable and green programs may offer better terms for rent or efficiency commitments. Model the cost of the commitment.
- Rate caps for floating rate loans add cost and time.

### Diligence additions
- Lender quote with program, term, sizing and fees.
- Required third-party reports and scope of required repairs.
- Property condition against agency standards.
- Rent roll and T-12 in the lender's format.
- Borrower and guarantor financial tests.
- Assumption terms, transfer rules and release prices for portfolios.
- Any affordability or use commitments tied to better pricing. Counsel to review.

### Red flags
- Underwritten NOI above what a lender will accept.
- Repair escrow too large for the equity plan.
- Prepayment cost ignored in an early exit.
- Floating rate with weak cap or no cap budget.
- Program fit unclear for a student, manufactured or single-family property.
- Recourse triggers missed in the loan documents.

### Exit effects
- Buyers often assume agency loans, which supports value if the rate is attractive.
- Prepayment cost can erase gains in an early sale. Show net proceeds with and without.
- Supplemental loans may shorten the hold or reduce flexibility. Show the effect.

## Using overlays in the model

- Add each program's cost and constraint as a named input, source-tagged and dated.
- Run the base case without the overlay benefit, then add it. Show both.
- Record every tax and legal assumption as Assumed, with an owner (CPA or counsel) and a date.
