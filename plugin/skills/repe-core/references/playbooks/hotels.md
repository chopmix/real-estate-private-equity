# Hotels playbook

Engine: operating. Revenue is driven by rooms sold and rate, not by leases. Underwrite the business and the real estate separately.

## 1. What it is

| Subtype | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Limited service | Few staff, rooms revenue above 90 percent of total, high margins, low capital per key |
| Select service | Light food and beverage, small meeting space, strong margins |
| Full service | Restaurants, banquets, meeting space, spa. Lower margins, heavy labor, higher capital needs |
| Extended stay | Kitchens, long stays, low turnover cost, steady occupancy |
| Resort and luxury | Seasonal, ancillary revenue, large operating complexity, destination risk |
| Independent or soft brand | No full franchise system. Weaker distribution, more freedom |

## 2. How income is produced

- Guests pay a nightly rate. Revenue is keys x 365 x occupancy x average daily rate, plus other departments.
- Three contracts sit between owner and guest: the franchise (brand) agreement, the management agreement and the loan.
- The owner keeps what is left after departmental costs, undistributed costs, fees, taxes, insurance and reserve.
- Value is the business plus the real estate. If the brand or manager leaves, much of the value leaves with it.

## 3. Key metrics

| Metric | How to compute | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| Occupancy | Rooms sold / rooms available | Volume. Read by month and day of week |
| ADR | Room revenue / rooms sold | Pricing power |
| RevPAR | Occupancy x ADR | Core top-line measure |
| RevPAR index | Hotel RevPAR / competitive set RevPAR x 100 | Share of the market. Below 100 means losing share |
| GOP margin | Gross operating profit / total revenue | Operating strength before fees and fixed charges |
| EBITDA less reserve | GOP less management fee, franchise fees, taxes, insurance, FF&E reserve | Owner cash flow. The number lenders size on |
| Flow-through | Change in profit / change in revenue | Tests whether growth reaches the bottom line |
| Cost per occupied room | Departmental cost / rooms sold | Control of labor and supplies |
| Value per key | Price / keys | Compare with replacement cost per key |
| Total cost to brand | Franchise fees plus loyalty and marketing fees, as percent of rooms revenue | Often larger than the headline royalty |

## 4. Revenue drivers

- Keys, occupancy by season and day of week, ADR by segment (transient, group, contract, wholesale, online agencies).
- Segment mix: corporate, leisure, group, extended. Each has its own rate and cancellation behavior.
- Distribution: brand reservation system, loyalty members, online agency share and commission cost.
- Other revenue: food and beverage, meeting space, parking, resort or destination fees, spa, retail, telecom.
- Demand generators: airport, hospital, university, convention center, corporate campus, events, leisure draw.

## 5. Expense drivers

- Rooms: housekeeping labor (largest line), laundry, supplies, commissions, reservation fees.
- Food and beverage: cost of sales and labor. Often loses money outside full service. Check how it is allocated.
- Undistributed: administrative, sales and marketing, utilities, repairs and maintenance, franchise fees.
- Management fee, usually a percent of revenue, sometimes with an incentive fee on profit.
- Property tax: many jurisdictions value hotels on income and reassess after a sale. Model the reassessed value. Hotel occupancy taxes are collected from guests and are not revenue.
- Insurance: coastal, storm and liability exposure raise cost. Obtain a quote.
- Labor: union status, minimum wage steps, benefit costs, staff turnover.

## 6. Capital needs

- FF&E reserve, usually a percent of revenue, rule of thumb, verify locally. Brand agreements set a minimum.
- Product improvement plan (PIP): the brand's required renovation, due on sale or change of ownership. Price it before bidding.
- Soft goods cycle (beds, carpet, furniture) and hard goods cycle (roof, elevators, HVAC, facade).
- Renovation downtime: displaced rooms lose revenue and may cut the loan's coverage.

## 7. Debt and lender view

- Lenders: banks, debt funds, life companies for strong assets, CMBS for stabilized assets. Hotels carry the highest risk premium of the main property types.
- Sizing: debt yield and DSCR usually bind, not LTV. Lenders size on trailing 12-month EBITDA less full reserve and a market management fee.
- Lenders look at RevPAR index, brand quality and manager quality.
- Quirks: lockbox with cash management, brand and manager comfort letters, reserve accounts for PIP, subordination of the management fee, recourse carve-outs, license and franchise assignment.

## 8. Screening quick tests

| Test | Pass looks like |
|------|-----------------|
| Value per key against replacement cost | Below new build cost, with a reason it is not cheaper to build |
| RevPAR index | At or above 100, or a clear fix |
| GOP and EBITDA margin | In line with the same service level. Explain any gap |
| Total cost to brand | Known, as percent of rooms revenue, and reasonable |
| Remaining franchise and management term | Long enough to cover the hold, or renewal terms are known |
| PIP cost per key | Included in the price |
| Debt yield at going-in | Meets the lender's minimum with room to spare |

## 9. Diligence additions

- Management agreement: term, fee, incentive fee, termination rights (without cause, for performance, on sale), key person, owner approval of budget, owner consent over capital spending.
- Franchise agreement: term, termination fee, liquidated damages, change of control and transfer rules, PIP schedule, area protection, performance test.
- Hotel benchmarking reports for the competitive set: three to five years of RevPAR index by month, plus the competitive set definition.
- Monthly operating statements for three years, budget vs actual, and the manager's forward booking pace.
- Brand inspection scores and guest satisfaction scores.
- Payroll by position, union agreements, benefit plans.
- Licenses: liquor, pool, elevator, fire, health. Check violations.
- Property condition report with room-by-room condition and a PIP estimate.
- Contracts: group business on the books, online agency contracts, corporate rates, equipment leases.
- Tax: occupancy tax compliance, property tax appeals, sales tax audits. Flag for the CPA.

## 10. Red flags

- RevPAR growth driven by rate only while occupancy slides, or the reverse.
- RevPAR index below 100 and falling.
- Seller's EBITDA that ignores the reserve, uses a fee below market, or excludes owner costs.
- Large near-term PIP with no price reduction.
- Management or franchise agreement that cannot be terminated on sale and has long term.
- One demand source (one employer, one event) above a quarter of room nights.
- Online agency share rising, direct share falling.
- Deferred maintenance in rooms, roof or mechanical systems.
- Review scores trending down, or repeated cleanliness and safety comments.
- New supply approved near the hotel.

## 11. Value levers

- Change the manager or the brand, if agreements allow.
- Revenue management: rate strategy, segment mix, direct booking.
- Reposition or renovate to move up the rate tier, tested against the competitive set.
- Cut costs: housekeeping model, food and beverage footprint, energy, shared services.
- Add rooms, meeting space or amenities where land and zoning allow.
- Renegotiate fees or term at renewal.

## 12. Local intelligence

- Online review sites and travel platforms: score trend, volume, owner response, repeated themes (cleanliness, noise, staff, safety). Read patterns, never name staff or reviewers.
- Competitive set pricing on the same dates, and online listing ranking.
- Supply: building permits, planning agendas and announced openings for competing hotels.
- Demand: airline traffic, convention bookings, event calendars, major employer news, university and hospital expansion.
- Short-term rental growth and local rules.

## 13. Model inputs to capture

- Keys, room types, year built, last renovation.
- Occupancy by year, ADR by year and segment, RevPAR index.
- Other revenue: food and beverage, meetings, parking, resort fee, other, as percent of rooms revenue.
- Departmental expense percent of revenue: rooms, food and beverage, other.
- Undistributed expense percent of revenue: administrative, sales and marketing, utilities, repairs.
- Management fee percent and incentive fee. Franchise fees by line.
- FF&E reserve percent by year. PIP amount and timing.
- Property tax on reassessed value, insurance, ground rent.
- Capital plan, renovation downtime, working capital.
- Exit cap rate, sale cost, and hold period.
- Debt: amount, rate, interest-only period, amortization, reserve requirements.

## 14. Site visit and management questions

- Walk guest rooms, a room not yet renovated, corridors, back of house, laundry and the roof.
- Ask the general manager what the next competitor opening would do to rate.
- Which segments are growing, which are weak, and why?
- What are the biggest guest complaints and how fast are they fixed?
- What is the staff turnover, and how many positions are open?
- What does the brand audit say, and when is the next one?
- What deferred items has the manager asked the owner to fund?
- What does the forward group and corporate pace look like?
- What does the manager earn in total, including fees charged to the hotel?
