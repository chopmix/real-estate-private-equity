# For-sale housing playbook

Engine: development layer with a sell-out. Condominiums, townhomes, single-family homes and finished or partly finished lots. There is no exit cap rate. Value comes from selling units for more than they cost.

## 1. What it is

| Subtype | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Condominium | Presales common, deposits held, long build, completion before closings |
| Townhome | Phased starts, sold as built or on contract |
| Single-family build for sale | Home builder model, spec or to order |
| Finished lot development | Sell lots to builders. Land, infrastructure, entitlements |
| Bulk lot or parcel sale | Sell to one buyer. Lower price, lower risk, faster |

## 2. How income is produced

- Revenue is sales proceeds when each unit or lot closes. Nothing else matters until closings begin.
- Profit is net sale revenue minus total cost. Total cost includes land, construction, soft costs, interest and sales costs.
- Equity is outstanding until the last unit closes. Speed of sell-out drives return as much as margin.
- Presales and deposits reduce risk and support the construction loan, but buyers can default or walk.
- Unsold inventory carries interest, taxes, insurance, association dues and carrying costs.

## 3. Key metrics

| Metric | How to compute | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| Net sale revenue | Gross sales less sales costs, concessions and closing costs | Cash that reaches the project |
| Profit margin | (Net sale revenue minus total cost) / net sale revenue | Cushion against price decline |
| Return on cost | Profit / total cost | Return before time |
| Levered IRR and multiple | From dated equity out and distributions in | Time-weighted return. Use XIRR |
| Price per SF | Sale price / saleable SF | Market comparison |
| Absorption | Units or lots sold per month | Pace and carry |
| Months of supply | Unsold units / monthly absorption | Time to sell out |
| Presale percent | Units under contract / total units | Risk reduction |
| Contract to close rate | Closings / contracts | Quality of presales |
| Breakeven price | Price at which profit is zero | Margin of safety |
| Cost to complete | Remaining budget plus overruns minus committed funding | Funding gap |

## 4. Revenue drivers

- Unit count, unit mix and size, view or floor premiums, lot sizes and premiums.
- Base price per SF and price escalation by phase. Test against recent closed sales, not asking prices.
- Absorption per period and seasonality.
- Concessions: closing cost help, rate buy-downs, upgrades, free parking. Show net price.
- Buyer financing: mortgage availability, rate sensitivity, investor share of buyers.
- Deposits and cancellations, and how deposits are held.
- Upgrades, options and lot premiums.

## 5. Expense drivers

- Construction costs: see capital needs.
- Sales and marketing: commissions, model unit, advertising, sales staff, signage, website.
- Closing costs: transfer taxes, title, legal, warranty.
- Carry on unsold units: property tax, insurance, utilities, association dues, loan interest.
- Association funding: developer pays dues on unsold units and may fund deficits. Check the budget.
- Warranty and construction defect reserve after closing. Condominiums carry the most exposure.
- Property tax on land and partly built projects. Check reassessment after completion.

## 6. Capital needs

- Budget by category: land, site and infrastructure, hard cost by unit type, soft cost, financing, sales and marketing, warranty reserve, contingency.
- Contingency: rule of thumb, verify locally. Size to design stage and build complexity.
- Equity, loan draw schedule and release prices: payment per unit sold to repay the loan.
- Interest and carry reserve through sell-out.
- Model unit and common area completion before first closing.
- Association startup fund and reserves, funded at closing or by developer.

## 7. Debt and lender view

- Lenders: banks for most construction and acquisition loans, debt funds and private lenders for lots and condos. Loans are shorter and cost more than for income property.
- Sizing: loan to cost and loan to sales value. Lenders also test presales and net proceeds after sales costs.
- Release prices: each closing repays a set share of the loan, usually more than the pro rata amount. Check the percent and any minimum price.
- Lender usually requires presale percent before funding for condominiums, deposit escrow, and completion testing.
- Guarantees: completion, carry, repayment, and recourse on cost overruns. Identify the guarantor. Flag for counsel.

## 8. Screening quick tests

| Test | Pass looks like |
|------|-----------------|
| Profit margin on net revenue | Meets the firm's minimum after a price decline case |
| Breakeven price vs comps | Well below recent closed sales |
| Absorption pace | Backed by closed sales of competing projects |
| Months of supply | Within what the loan term and carry reserve allow |
| Presales and deposits | Meaningful, with contract quality tested |
| Release price | Clears loan without squeezing equity |

## 9. Diligence additions

- Entitlements, plat approvals, and subdivision or condominium filing requirements. Flag for counsel.
- Condominium documents: declaration, bylaws, budget, public offering statement, approval by state agencies, rules on deposits and rescission.
- Purchase contract forms, deposit terms, closing deadlines, default and remedies, delivery dates.
- Presale file: buyer names kept private, deposit amount, financing contingency, investor share, cancellation rate.
- Pricing study by a local broker with closed comps and active listings.
- Construction contract, contractor review, budget review by a third party, schedule.
- Warranty and defect: coverage, claim history of contractor, insurance for construction defects.
- Association budget, developer subsidy, reserve funding.
- Environmental and geotechnical reports, utilities, off-site costs.

## 10. Red flags

- Margin that depends on price growth during the build.
- Absorption plan faster than any competing project achieved.
- Presales with small deposits, high investor share or weak financing contingency.
- Cancellations rising.
- Many competing deliveries in the same period.
- Release prices that force a deep discount at the end.
- Loan matures before projected sell-out.
- Association with no reserves or with a dues budget that is too low to be credible.
- Construction defect history, or condominium in a market with strict defect rules.

## 11. Value levers

- Reprice by phase using actual absorption signals.
- Hold back premium units for later release.
- Presell or bulk sell to reduce carry.
- Reduce cost through design and early buyout.
- Phase construction to match sales.
- Add upgrades and options with real margin.
- Negotiate release prices and extension terms.

## 12. Local intelligence

- Building permits, plats and planning agendas: competing projects, entitlement news and public hearings.
- Competing sell-outs: number of units, price, pace of absorption, concessions, and months of supply. Track sales recorded in county deed records, not just ads.
- Closed sales and median price trends from county records and local brokers.
- Mortgage and insurance availability for the building type, and local transfer taxes.
- Local employment and in-migration news, and supply of resale homes.

## 13. Model inputs to capture

- Unit or lot count, mix, size, saleable SF.
- Price per unit or per SF by type and phase, price growth, premiums, concessions.
- Absorption per period by phase, presale percent and timing, cancellation rate.
- Deposits: percent, timing, how held, release to construction.
- Sales costs: commissions, marketing, closing costs, warranty reserve, as percent of revenue.
- Budget by category, contingency percent, schedule, draw schedule.
- Carry on unsold units: tax, insurance, dues, interest.
- Loan: amount, rate, release price percent, fees, extension terms.

## 14. Site visit and management questions

- Visit the sales center and model, and visit competing projects as a buyer.
- Ask the sales lead who the buyers are: owner occupants, investors, local or out of town.
- Ask how many contracts have been signed, how many cancelled, and why.
- Ask how deposits are held and when buyers can walk.
- Ask what the price would be if sales paced half as fast.
- Ask what the loan requires for each closing and for extension.
- Ask who bears construction defect and warranty risk.
- Ask what the plan is for the last ten percent of units.
