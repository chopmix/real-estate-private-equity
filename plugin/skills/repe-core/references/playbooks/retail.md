# Playbook: Retail

Engine: `lease` (lease-by-lease). Read with `metrics-and-formulas.md` and `standards.md`.

## 1. What it is

| Subtype | Description | Main risk |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| Grocery-anchored | Supermarket anchor with shop space | Anchor lease terms, competing stores |
| Strip / neighborhood | Small tenants, services and food | Small tenant churn, credit |
| Power center | Big-box tenants, wide trade area | Box closures, co-tenancy |
| Mall | Enclosed, anchors plus inline | Anchor loss, sales decline, capex |
| Single-tenant pad | Pad site with one tenant, often drive-through | Credit and term, see net-lease playbook |

## 2. How income is produced

- Lease by lease. Anchors pay low rent per SF on long terms. Inline shops pay more per SF on shorter terms.
- Structures: NNN with CAM, tax and insurance recoveries is common. Check caps and exclusions.
- Percentage rent applies above a breakpoint. Treat it as upside, not base.
- Co-tenancy clauses let tenants reduce rent or leave if an anchor or occupancy threshold fails.
- Exclusives and use restrictions limit what you can lease to others.
- Anchors may own their box or lease it. Know the control rights.

## 3. Key metrics

| Metric | How to compute | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| WALT | Remaining term weighted by rent | Lock-in |
| Rollover by year | Rent expiring each year / total rent | Re-leasing cost |
| Mark to market | Market rent / in-place rent minus 1 | Upside or downside |
| Occupancy cost | Tenant rent and charges / tenant sales | Health of the tenant and rent room |
| Sales per SF | Reported tenant sales / SF | Strength of the center |
| Anchor share of rent | Anchor rent / total rent | Dependence |
| Inline vs anchor occupancy | Occupied SF by tenant class | Tests co-tenancy risk |
| Net effective rent | PV of rent less free rent, TI, commissions | Compare deals |
| Recovery ratio | Recoveries / recoverable expenses | Leakage test |

## 4. Revenue drivers

- Contract rent and bumps (fixed steps, or percent increases at renewal options).
- Percentage rent above breakpoints.
- Rollover: renewal probability, downtime and market rent. Small shops churn faster.
- Recoveries: CAM, tax and insurance, with caps and gross-up clauses.
- Pad and outparcel ground leases.

## 5. Expense drivers

- Property tax. Model reassessment on sale where the jurisdiction reassesses.
- Insurance, including liability for open parking areas.
- CAM: landscaping, snow, sweeping, lighting, security.
- Repairs and maintenance on roof, paving and signage.
- Management fee as a percent of EGI.
- Reserves below NOI for roofs, paving and facade.

## 6. Capital needs

- TI for inline shops, with white-box deliveries.
- Anchor re-tenanting or box splitting costs.
- Leasing commissions, with outside broker splits.
- Parking lot repaving, lighting and facade refresh.
- Redevelopment of excess land or boxes.

## 7. Debt and lender view

- Lenders: banks, life companies, CMBS, debt funds.
- Grocery-anchored often gets the best terms. Malls and weak power centers get less leverage.
- Debt yield and DSCR usually bind.
- Lenders look at anchor term, sales and co-tenancy exposure.
- Rule of thumb, verify locally: lenders require reserves and sweeps before an anchor expiry.

## 8. Screening quick tests

| Test | Pass looks like |
|------|-----------------|
| Anchor term | Long remaining term with options |
| Anchor sales | Healthy sales per SF and low occupancy cost |
| Co-tenancy exposure | Rent at risk if an anchor leaves is quantified |
| In-line occupancy cost | Shops can afford rent growth |
| Basis vs replacement cost | Below cost to build |
| Trade area | Growth in households and traffic |

## 9. Diligence additions

- Abstract every lease: exclusives, co-tenancy, use clauses, go-dark rights, radius restrictions, termination and percentage rent.
- Read the reciprocal easement agreements and anchor control rights.
- Collect tenant sales reports for percentage rent and occupancy cost.
- Verify CAM reconciliations and audit rights.
- Tenant financials for anchors and national tenants.
- Check parking ratios against zoning and lease promises.
- Traffic counts and access, curb cuts and signage rights.
- Property condition report.

## 10. Red flags

- Anchor with short term, no options or a go-dark right.
- Co-tenancy triggers close to current occupancy.
- Occupancy costs high relative to sales.
- Inline tenants on month-to-month or with unpaid rent.
- Reliance on percentage rent for NOI.
- Competing center or anchor opening nearby.
- Exclusives that block leasing of vacant space.

## 11. Value levers

- Renew anchors early with term in exchange for limited concessions.
- Replace weak tenants with stronger ones in the same box.
- Mark below-market shop rents to market at rollover.
- Add pads and outparcel ground leases.
- Split big boxes where demand supports it.
- Redevelop excess land, subject to REA and zoning.

## 12. Local intelligence

- Tenant news first: store closure lists, bankruptcy filings, credit downgrades, relocations and franchise failures.
- Anchor news: format changes, closures, new stores nearby.
- Government lease actions affecting service tenants.
- New supply: new centers, pad approvals and big-box openings.
- Sublease and dark box listings.
- Traffic projects and road changes affecting access.

## 13. Model inputs to capture

| Input | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Tenant, suite, SF, class | Anchor, junior anchor, shop, pad |
| Lease start, expiry, options | Include notice dates |
| Base rent per SF and bumps | Fixed steps or percent |
| Lease structure | NNN, modified gross, gross |
| Percentage rent | Breakpoint and rate |
| Tenant sales per SF | Last three years |
| Market rent per SF | By class and size |
| Renewal probability | Tenant-level |
| Downtime months | By class |
| Free rent months | New vs renewal |
| TI per SF | New vs renewal |
| Leasing commission | New vs renewal |
| Recoveries, admin fee, caps | Per SF |
| Co-tenancy and go-dark terms | Trigger and remedy |
| Opex lines, tax reassessment, reserves | Tax, insurance, CAM, management, per SF |

## 14. Site visit and management questions

- Visit at peak and quiet hours. Count cars and foot traffic.
- Walk every unit. Note closed, dark and struggling shops.
- Ask which tenants have asked for rent relief.
- Ask the anchor manager about store performance and plans.
- Ask about exclusives and disputes with tenants.
- Ask how tenants are paying and who is behind on rent.
