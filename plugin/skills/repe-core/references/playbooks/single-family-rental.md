# Playbook: Single-Family Rental (including Build-to-Rent)

Engine: `unit`. Income comes from individual homes or townhomes on 12 month leases. Scale depends on management systems, not on the building.

## 1. What it is

| Subtype | Description | Underwriting focus |
|---------|-------------|--------------------|
| Scattered-site portfolio | Homes bought one by one across a metro | Property-level variance, management density, turn cost |
| Build-to-rent (BTR) community | Purpose-built detached or townhome rental neighborhood | Lease-up pace, construction budget, amenity cost |
| Bulk portfolio acquisition | Pool of homes bought from one seller | Data quality, condition variance, title and tax per home |
| Renovated flip-and-hold | Homes bought, renovated, then rented | Renovation scope, yield on cost, rent proof |
| Small-balance rental fund | A set of homes funded under one structure | Reporting, lender covenants, entity structure |

## 2. How income is produced

- Monthly rent on 12 to 24 month leases, with a single household per home.
- Tenants stay longer than apartment residents, so turnover cost is lower per home but each turn is larger.
- Other income: pet rent, fees, smart-home and tech packages, lawn service, filter programs.
- No on-site staff in scattered sites. Management is routed through dispatch, field teams and vendors.
- Engine inputs: home types by bed and bath, counts, SF, in-place and market rent, renovation premium and pace.

## 3. Key metrics

| Metric | How to compute | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| Net yield | NOI / all-in cost per home | Core return test |
| Economic occupancy | Collected rent / GPR | Collections matter more than occupancy alone |
| Turnover rate | Move-outs / homes | Drives vacancy and make-ready cost |
| Turn cost per home | Make-ready cost / homes turned | Larger and more variable than apartments |
| Days to re-lease | Average days vacant between tenants | Vacancy driver |
| Maintenance per home | Repairs and maintenance / homes | Age and system condition |
| Renewal rent spread | Renewal rent / prior rent minus 1 | Indicates pricing power |
| Cost per home | Total cost / homes | Compare with replacement cost and resale value |
| Homes per field employee | Homes / maintenance headcount | Management efficiency |

## 4. Revenue drivers

- Local rent growth and household formation, including move-in demand for single-family.
- Rent for each home type, with proof from leases.
- Renewal pricing and tenant retention.
- Other income programs and their legal limits.
- Occupancy and collection speed. Eviction timeline in the jurisdiction.
- For BTR: lease-up pace, concessions, and premium for new construction.

## 5. Expense drivers

- Property tax: reassessed on purchase in many jurisdictions. Check by county, since rates vary.
- Insurance: per-home policies or blanket coverage. Roof age, hail and wind exposure.
- Homeowners association dues, where applicable.
- Repairs and maintenance, turn, and make-ready. Age and system condition drive the line.
- Property management fee, plus leasing fee and renewal fee.
- Landscaping, pest control, utilities during vacancy.
- Technology and dispatch costs that scale with the portfolio.

## 6. Capital needs

- Roofs, HVAC, water heaters, plumbing, foundation, flooring and appliances on a cycle.
- Initial renovation scope per home, with a cost cap.
- For BTR: site work, amenities, fencing, landscaping and warranty period repairs.
- Reserves per home, scaled by age. Older homes need more.
- Technology rollout (smart locks, thermostats) if it supports pricing.

## 7. Debt and lender view

- Lenders: agency lenders (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) for qualifying single-family rental programs, banks, debt funds, securitized single-family loans.
- Usually binding test: DSCR or debt yield on lender-adjusted NOI. Many programs size to a per-home value or appraisal.
- Quirks: portfolio release prices, cross-collateralization, geographic concentration limits, minimum home counts, appraisal by sample, and tax and insurance escrows.
- BTR construction loans size to cost with a completion guaranty and lease-up tests.

## 8. Screening quick tests

- Net yield against borrowing cost, and against apartment yield in the same market.
- All-in cost per home against replacement cost and resale value.
- Rent-to-cost ratio, labeled rule of thumb, verify locally, compared with the local norm.
- Maintenance and turn cost in the T-12 against home age.
- Tax reassessment impact on NOI.
- Exit view: bulk portfolio, individual home sale, or institutional buyer, and the discount for each.

## 9. Diligence additions

- Home-level file: address, year built, SF, beds and baths, condition, rent, lease dates, tax and HOA.
- Inspection of a statistically meaningful sample, plus every home with a known issue.
- Title, liens, code violations, and pending assessments on each home.
- Lease audit: terms, deposits, pet policy, resident utility responsibilities.
- Rent versus market for each home type, using leased comps.
- Insurance quotes by region, and claims history.
- Property manager contract, fee, and termination terms. Technology stack and data access.
- Tenant payment history and bad debt by home.
- Flood zone, wildfire, hail, and foundation risk screening.
- For BTR: entitlements, plat status, utility capacity, builder warranty, and construction contract.
- Fair housing and local rental licensing compliance, reviewed by counsel.

## 10. Red flags

- Homes spread thinly across many suburbs with no local management density.
- Rent roll shows many below-market legacy leases with no plan to reprice.
- Condition of roofs, HVAC and foundations missing from the data.
- Bulk portfolio with poor data and no home-level file.
- High turnover with high turn cost.
- Tax reassessment ignored in a high-tax county.
- Insurance quotes far above the trailing cost.
- Many homes in HOAs with rental restrictions.
- BTR construction behind schedule or builder concentration risk.
- Review patterns showing slow repairs and poor response.

## 11. Value levers

- Raise management density and reduce cost per home with local teams.
- Improve renewal pricing and retention.
- Renovate on a tested scope and pace.
- Add services and other income that tenants will pay for.
- Reduce insurance, tax and turn costs through bulk programs and appeals.
- Sell select homes to owner-occupants where the price beats rental value.
- For BTR: deliver on time, lease up quickly, then refinance or sell to a stabilized buyer.
- Aggregate small portfolios to a scale that an institutional buyer will pay for.

## 12. Local intelligence

- Reviews: read tenant and manager reviews for repair response, deposit disputes and fee complaints. Track trends. Do not name staff or tenants.
- New supply: BTR communities, builder permits, and land sales in the submarket.
- Employer news: major hires, relocations and layoffs. Single-family demand follows household income.
- Public data: county tax, flood maps, school ratings, crime maps, code enforcement, HOA rules.
- Broker and listing data for rents and days on market. Label as opinion.
- Rent comps from listings, with concessions noted.

## 13. Model inputs to capture

- Home types: bed and bath count, number, average SF, in-place and market rent.
- Renovation program: homes per year, cost per home, premium, downtime.
- Vacancy, concessions, bad debt, turnover and days to re-lease.
- Other income by line.
- Expense lines: taxes, insurance, HOA, repairs and maintenance, turn, management and leasing fees, landscaping, administrative.
- Tax reassessment value and millage.
- Reserves per home by age. Capital plan by year.
- Rent and expense growth, exit approach (bulk or individual), sale costs and disposition discount.
- Debt terms, release prices and escrows.

## 14. Site visit and management questions

- Drive a sample of homes and their streets, not only the best ones.
- Ask how many homes are vacant, in renovation, or in eviction.
- What is the average days to re-lease and why?
- What are the top five repair categories by cost?
- How is dispatch routed and what is the response time?
- How are rents set and who approves renewals?
- What proportion of vendors are in-house?
- How do you handle HOA violations and city notices?
- What does the tenant file look like for a typical home?
- For BTR: when is the next phase, and what controls the pace?
