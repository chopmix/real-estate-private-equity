# Playbook: Medical Office

Engine: `lease` (lease-by-lease). Read with `metrics-and-formulas.md` and `standards.md`.

## 1. What it is

| Subtype | Description | Main risk |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| On-campus MOB | Building on or tied to a hospital campus, often ground leased | Hospital health, ground lease terms |
| Off-campus MOB | Near hospitals but independent | Physician demand and competing sites |
| Ambulatory / outpatient | Imaging, dialysis, surgery centers, urgent care | Specialized buildout, operator credit |
| Physician practice building | Small private groups, one or several floors | Practice turnover, retirement |
| Single-tenant medical | One operator on a long lease | See net-lease playbook for credit and residual |

## 2. How income is produced

- Tenant-by-tenant leases. Terms are often longer than general office, with renewal options.
- Structures: NNN or modified gross are common. Check who pays what.
- Hospital-sponsored leases may carry rent support, guarantees or master leases. Read each.
- Ground leases on campus: rent, term, reversion and leasehold financing limits.
- Tenants invest heavily in buildout, which lifts renewal odds.

## 3. Key metrics

| Metric | How to compute | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| WALT | Remaining term weighted by rent | Longer terms are a feature here |
| Rollover by year | Rent expiring each year / total rent | Re-leasing is costly |
| Renewal rate | Renewed SF / expiring SF over past cycles | Test of stickiness |
| Mark to market | Market rent / in-place rent minus 1 | Rent bumps and resets |
| Hospital affiliation share | Rent from system-affiliated tenants / total rent | Credit and referral link |
| Tenant concentration | Top 5 tenants as share of rent | Group departure risk |
| Net effective rent | PV of rent less free rent, TI and commissions, per SF | TI is heavy here |
| Occupancy cost | Rent and charges as share of practice revenue | Affordability |

## 4. Revenue drivers

- Contract rent, bumps (usually fixed annual percent) and reset rights.
- Rollover: renewal probability is higher than office, with lower downtime.
- Recoveries under NNN or modified gross with expense caps.
- Hospital rent support and master lease terms.
- Ground rent steps, if on campus.

## 5. Expense drivers

- Property tax. Check exemptions that end on sale or when tenants change.
- Insurance, with higher liability and medical waste exposure.
- Utilities, with high HVAC loads and 24-hour operation in some suites.
- Janitorial to medical standards. Biohazard and waste handling.
- Management fee as a percent of EGI.
- Repairs: plumbing, medical gas, fume systems.
- Ground rent if applicable.
- Reserves below NOI for roof, mechanical and elevators.

## 6. Capital needs

- TI is high: plumbing, power, shielding, cabinets and specialty fit-out.
- Leasing commissions for new vs renewal tenants.
- Imaging and surgical suite buildouts can cost many times a standard suite.
- Base building upgrades: backup power, medical gas, accessibility, and life safety.
- Parking structure repairs on campus assets.

## 7. Debt and lender view

- Lenders: banks, life companies, debt funds, agency-style programs for some borrowers.
- Debt yield and DSCR usually bind.
- Lenders value hospital affiliation, long WALT and sticky tenants.
- On-campus loans on ground-leased land need leasehold mortgage rights.
- Rule of thumb, verify locally: lenders give credit for affiliation only when a lease or covenant supports it.
- Watch master lease and rent support terms for step-downs.

## 8. Screening quick tests

| Test | Pass looks like |
|------|-----------------|
| Affiliation | Hospital or system tenants with documented ties |
| WALT | Term supports the hold with fair renewal options |
| Practice concentration | No single group above a tolerable share of rent |
| Basis vs replacement cost | Below cost to build, including specialized fit-out |
| Ground lease (if any) | Long remaining term, clear reversion, mortgageable |
| Tenant spend | Heavy buildouts in place suggest renewals |

## 9. Diligence additions

- Lease abstracts with exclusives, relocation rights, early termination and TI amortization.
- Review hospital agreements: master lease, rent support, ROFR and parking.
- Check Medicare-related and referral-law structure of rents with counsel (fair market value).
- Tenant financials for any group above 10 percent of rent. Ask about payor mix.
- Licenses, certificates of need and permits for imaging and surgery tenants.
- Physician age and succession plans for the largest practices.
- Property condition: medical gas, backup power, plumbing, HVAC and waste handling.
- Ground lease review by counsel if on campus.

## 10. Red flags

- Rent set above fair market value, which creates regulatory exposure (counsel).
- Hospital system under financial stress or planning to consolidate sites.
- Anchor group aging out with no buyer.
- Rent support that steps down before leases roll.
- Specialized suites with no alternate user.
- Short ground lease term remaining.
- Competing campus building delivering nearby.

## 11. Value levers

- Early renewal and extension of strong tenants.
- Mark rents to market at reset and bumps.
- Fill vacancy with groups tied to the anchor hospital.
- Add ambulatory uses or imaging on ground floors.
- Extend or restructure a ground lease.

## 12. Local intelligence

- Tenant news first: practice mergers, physician departures, group sales, payor changes, closures and credit events.
- Hospital system announcements: new outpatient centers, service line moves, ownership changes.
- Government lease actions and public health program changes that affect tenants.
- New supply: competing MOBs, freestanding emergency and urgent care sites.
- Sublease or dark space in nearby medical buildings.
- State licensing and certificate of need filings.

## 13. Model inputs to capture

| Input | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Tenant, suite, SF, specialty | One row per lease |
| Lease start, expiry, options | Include notice dates |
| Base rent per SF and bumps | Fixed or CPI |
| Lease structure | NNN, modified gross, gross |
| Market rent per SF | By suite type |
| Renewal probability | Higher for built-out suites |
| Downtime months | New vs renewal |
| TI per SF | New vs renewal, by specialty |
| Leasing commission | New vs renewal |
| Recoveries and caps | Per SF |
| Rent support or master lease | Amount and end date |
| Ground rent | Steps and reversion |
| Opex lines | Tax, insurance, utilities, janitorial, R&M, management |
| Tax reassessment | Year and value after sale |
| Reserves per SF | Mechanical and structure |

## 14. Site visit and management questions

- Walk suites with each large tenant. Look at equipment, waiting rooms and patient flow.
- Ask which groups are growing, merging or selling.
- Ask how the hospital sees the building in its plans.
- Ask about open TI, disputes and CAM audits.
- Ask which specialties left and why.
- Check life safety, backup power and medical gas servicing.
